What Actually Happens During a footscan® Assessment

"Footscan" gets used loosely to mean anything from a quick static print of your foot to a full gait lab. At Footscan Centre Ponteland, when we say footscan® we mean a specific, well-established piece of technology — a high-speed pressure plate developed by RSscan, now part of Materialise — and it's worth knowing exactly what it does before your appointment, so the results make sense the first time you see them.

The plate itself

The scan happens on a pressure plate embedded with thousands of resistive sensors — a typical footscan® plate has up to 48,384 of them, packed in tightly enough to register 11.1 sensors per square centimetre. As you stand or walk across it, each sensor measures the exact force and pressure at that point, up to 500 times a second on the higher-end systems we use. That combination — spatial resolution plus speed — is what separates it from a simple ink footprint or a phone app: it's capturing a genuine time series of how load moves through your foot, not a single snapshot.

Static vs. dynamic: why both matter

Your appointment covers two distinct measurements, and they tell you different things.

  • Static scan. You stand still on the plate. This shows how your weight distributes across your foot at rest — useful for spotting structural things like a collapsed arch or an asymmetry between your left and right foot that you'd never notice just standing in front of a mirror.
  • Dynamic scan. You walk (or run, for a sports assessment) across the plate at a normal pace, usually several times to get a consistent reading. This is where footscan® actually earns its reputation — it captures the full gait cycle, showing exactly when and where pressure peaks as your foot rolls from heel strike through to toe-off.

A static scan on its own can be misleading. Plenty of people who look "fine" standing still develop clear problems the moment they start moving — overpronation that only shows up mid-stride, or a delayed heel-to-toe transition that's driving knee pain. That's the whole reason dynamic, not just static, measurement matters.

What the results actually show you

You'll see a colour-mapped image of your foot — usually a spectrum from blue and green (light pressure) through to orange and red (high pressure), similar to the pressure map you can see above. We'll walk through it with you rather than just handing over a printout: where your pressure peaks are, whether left and right match up, and — most usefully — how the timing of pressure through your stride compares to a typical, efficient gait pattern. Reliability studies on the footscan® platform have shown consistent, repeatable results across sessions, which matters if you're using a scan to track whether an intervention (new footwear, exercises, orthotics) is actually working over time.

Who it's for

You don't need to be a runner or have an obvious injury to get value from a footscan® assessment. We see it used for:

  • Investigating knee, hip or lower-back pain that might be linked to how you walk
  • Understanding recurring foot pain that hasn't responded to generic advice
  • A baseline before a Phits Orthotics fitting, since your orthotic design is built directly from this data
  • Runners and athletes wanting to reduce injury risk or improve efficiency
